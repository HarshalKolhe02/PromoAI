# PART 1 — UNDERSTAND THE ASSIGNMENT

The project is based on the following exact problem statement:

"Build a tool that reads a microdrama screenplay and finds the best promos in it.

Today an editor watches every episode and picks promo moments by hand, one show at a time, and new shows are released every week.

A promo is a 20 to 60-second clip taken from the show itself and posted on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube to pull new viewers in.

A good promo:
1. Opens on a hook: something in the first 3 seconds that makes a scrolling viewer stop, such as a slap, a reveal or a shocking line.
2. Ends on a cliffhanger: it cuts at an unresolved moment, so the viewer wants to know what happens next.
3. Makes sense independently to someone who has never seen the show.

A promo is usually one continuous stretch but can combine multiple moments, from one episode or across several episodes.

The required tool must:
- Find promos across all episodes.
- Give the episode and start/end timestamps of every segment, in order.
- Explain the hook.
- Explain the cliffhanger.
- Provide one line explaining why each promo works.
- Rank the promos and explain which should be posted first.
- Check quality and identify where the tool picks badly.

The tool only identifies promotional moments and timestamps. It does not edit videos, add music or add text overlays."

Treat these requirements as non-negotiable.

Do not turn this project into a generic screenplay summariser or a script-generation tool.

The central deliverable is a reliable PROMO DISCOVERY AND RANKING SYSTEM.

# PART 4 — PROMO DISCOVERY ENGINE

This is the most important component.

Build a multi-stage analysis pipeline instead of sending the entire screenplay to one LLM and asking it to find promos.

The system should use AI for semantic understanding and ordinary Python code for deterministic validation, calculations and ranking.

## Stage 1: Episode understanding

For every episode, extract:

- Episode number and title.
- Characters.
- Character relationships.
- Locations.
- Important events.
- Conflicts.
- Secrets and revelations.
- Emotional confrontations.
- Betrayals.
- Threats.
- Unexpected appearances.
- Major decisions.
- Unresolved questions.
- Important story transitions.

Create a structured episode summary and character/story context.

The summary must be used for contextual analysis, not as a replacement for the original screenplay.

## Stage 2: Candidate moment detection

Scan every episode for potential promotional moments.

Look for:
- Physical confrontations.
- Slaps and fights.
- Unexpected entrances.
- Shocking revelations.
- Confessions.
- Betrayals.
- Accusations.
- Emotional breakdowns.
- Threats.
- Unexpected romantic developments.
- Secrets being exposed.
- Dramatic decisions.
- Suspenseful dialogue.
- Unexpected twists.
- Moments where the viewer learns something important.
- Moments where a character discovers something significant.

Do not select every dramatic moment automatically.

A good promotional candidate must be understandable and interesting to a person who has never seen the series.

Identify candidate start and end points using the original scene and dialogue timestamps.

## Stage 3: Hook detection

For every candidate, evaluate the first three seconds of the promo.

A hook should create an immediate reason to stop scrolling.

Examples:
- A shocking statement.
- A slap or confrontation.
- An unexpected revelation.
- A surprising visual action described in the screenplay.
- A threatening statement.
- An emotional reaction.
- An unexpected discovery.

Evaluate:
- Is something interesting happening immediately?
- Does the opening create curiosity?
- Is the opening emotionally compelling?
- Does it need too much previous context?
- Does the viewer understand what is happening?

A promo that only becomes interesting after 10 seconds should not receive a strong hook score.

The opening three seconds must be evaluated against the actual beginning of the selected promo, not against a later scene in it.

## Stage 4: Cliffhanger detection

Evaluate the ending of each candidate.

Look for:
- An unanswered question.
- An unresolved confrontation.
- A sudden revelation.
- An interrupted action.
- An unexpected discovery.
- A character about to make a major decision.
- An impending consequence.
- An unresolved threat.
- A dramatic twist that raises another question.

A promo should end at the moment of maximum unresolved tension.

Do not select an ending that already explains everything or resolves the conflict completely.

Explain exactly what remains unresolved.

## Stage 5: Standalone comprehensibility

A promotional clip must make sense to a viewer who has never watched the show.

Evaluate:
- Can the viewer understand who is involved?
- Is the central conflict understandable?
- Is enough context provided?
- Are unexplained names or relationships confusing?
- Does the clip depend heavily on previous episodes?
- Does the sequence communicate its own mini-story?

If a candidate needs additional context, identify exactly what is missing.

Do not automatically reject all context-dependent scenes. A small amount of understandable contextual information is acceptable.

## Stage 6: Duration validation

The total duration of every promo must be between 20 and 60 seconds inclusive.

For a continuous promo:

Total duration = End timestamp - Start timestamp.

For a multi-segment promo:

Total duration = Sum of all individual segment durations.

Validate this using Python, never by trusting an LLM's duration estimate.

Reject or flag candidates outside the permitted duration.

Also validate that:
- Start timestamps precede end timestamps.
- Timestamps belong to the correct episode.
- Segment ordering is clear.
- Segments do not overlap incorrectly.
- No segment has an impossible timestamp.
- The final duration is calculated correctly.

## Stage 7: Multi-segment promo discovery

Support both:

A. Continuous promo:
One uninterrupted section of an episode.

B. Multi-segment promo:
Two or more screenplay moments combined into one promotional sequence.

Multi-segment promos can use:
- Multiple scenes from one episode.
- Multiple episodes.
- Different episodes where the combined sequence creates a coherent promotional story.

However, do not combine scenes just to reach the duration requirement.

A multi-segment promo must have a logical progression:
Hook → Context / Conflict → Escalation → Cliffhanger.

Every segment must contribute meaningfully.

Preserve the correct chronological order within each episode. If segments span episodes, explain their order and any editorial sequencing assumption.

Never claim the clip is continuous when it is not.

For multi-segment promos, show each segment's episode, start time, end time and duration separately.

# PART 5 — PROMO SCORING AND RANKING

Create a transparent, configurable ranking system.

Do not simply ask the LLM to rank candidates without explaining its criteria.

Implement a deterministic weighted scoring framework.

Use the following initial weights:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Opening hook strength | 25 |
| Cliffhanger strength | 25 |
| Standalone comprehensibility | 20 |
| Emotional intensity / dramatic impact | 15 |
| Narrative curiosity / desire to know what happens next | 10 |
| Segment coherence and pacing | 5 |
| Total | 100 |

Each dimension should receive a score from 0 to 10.

Calculate the weighted score in Python.

Formula:

Final Score =
(Hook × 2.5) +
(Cliffhanger × 2.5) +
(Standalone × 2.0) +
(Emotional Impact × 1.5) +
(Curiosity × 1.0) +
(Coherence × 0.5)

The resulting score must be out of 100.

These are initial product assumptions. Document that they are heuristic weights, not scientifically validated measures of audience conversion.

## Hard eligibility checks

A candidate must not rank as a fully eligible promo if:
- Its duration is below 20 seconds or above 60 seconds.
- Its timestamps are invalid.
- It has no identifiable opening hook.
- It has no meaningful unresolved ending.
- It cannot be understood independently.

Keep rejected or uncertain candidates in a separate review section when useful.

Do not inflate a candidate's score to compensate for a failed hard requirement.

## Ranking output

Show:
- Rank.
- Promo ID.
- Episode or episodes.
- Total duration.
- Final score.
- Hook score.
- Cliffhanger score.
- Standalone score.
- Confidence.
- Why it works.
- Reason it should be posted early or later.

Explain the top three recommendations.

Also provide:
- Best overall promo.
- Strongest hook.
- Strongest cliffhanger.
- Best standalone promo.
- Best multi-segment promo.
- Candidates requiring human review.

Avoid duplicate or nearly identical promos dominating the top results.

Implement overlap and similarity checks to detect candidates that reuse almost the same screenplay moments.
